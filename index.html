01　Paralegal

Role overview

Paralegals are legal assistants who support solicitors in their professions by carrying out legal research, managing case files, drafting and preparing legal documents, and giving legal advice to clients.

Responsibilities

Essentially, paralegals have legal training within the field, however they do not cover services offered by solicitors, barristers or chartered legal executives such as litigation and mediation, as these require further training; though they do, in fact, work across multiple areas of law covering a range services in areas including property, family law, employment law and commercial law.

Routes into the role

There is not just one singular route into becoming a paralegal, which makes it easier and flexible for people from all kinds of backgrounds to get into this career pathway. Additionally, there is no requirement for a law degree to be a paralegal as employers value practical legal knowledge and capability in the workplace much more. Options into accessing this particular job role varies as there are paralegal apprenticeships at level 3 as well as college qualifications such as T levels in subjects such as legal services which makes this job accessible.

02　Legal administrator

Role overview

They work with solicitors generally keeping up with core tasks helping everything flow on a day to day basis. 03　Court reporter

Role overview

Court reporters are highly trained professionals who convert spoken words in the court into information that can be read and preserved. They generally capture the words spoken in a court proceeding and prepare this into a transcript that can be kept for further use on the case which also helps safeguard the legal process.They speed up time for the courts and allow the process to flow more smoothly; and dueto modern day technology some court reporters also provide real-time access to what is being said during a trial. Technology means that court reporters no longer have to type up everything that is said in court, as this is regulated by machinery these days, though technology is not always reliable meaning their job role is still highly important.

Responsibilities

Essentially, they have to ensure they are listening carefully to everything that is said, read back the transcripts and correct any mistakes, and draft a final copy of the court proceeding which means they are responsible for ensuring that records of the hearing can be understood and interpreted in the exact way they were in court.

Routes into the role

To become a court reporter, it is necessary to have a good understanding in English grammar, though no specific degree is required for this, again. This job role is more skills focused than academic qualification based, meaning it is accessible for anyone. Background knowledge for law is not required either, however students who study law at college have a perfect base for becoming a court reporter.

04　Title examiner

Role overview

A title examiner is a professional job within the legal industry and it looks at scrutinizing the history and legitimacy of property titles; they essentially specialise in property law and documentation where they use this knowledge to look at the legal standing of a property to identify any legal obstacles such as liens, disputes or claims.

Responsibilities

Within this role, they scrutinise property records ensuring it is free from legal obstacles, search through public records and legal documents in order to trace the history of the property's ownership, and collaborate with lawyers, real estate agents and insurance companies to resolve any issues whilst making recommendations for clearing the issues.

Routes into the role

In order to become a title examiner, no degree is essential and you can get into this straight from college. Relevant subjects to have studied does include law, though this is not essential as long as there is background knowledge present of the type of stuff the role includes, which can be done through research instead of academics. Again, there are multiple routes into getting into this role, including the apprenticeship route such as a level 3 paralegal apprenticeship or a level 4 conveyancing technician apprenticeship at a law firm; or even just getting work experience in a law firm focusing on property law. This job is more skills based which makes it reachable for most people as skills can be learned easily rather than needing to have a degree.

05　Legal cashier

Role overview

A legal cashier is a finance professional who manages day to day financial transactions, accounting and bookkeeping for law firms, ensuring that financial elements run smoothly.

Responsibilities

Their responsibilities include processing client and office transactions which looks at making sure the payments in the firm are correctly set up and recorded, carrying out reconciliations of bank statements against financial records, complying with the law society of Scotland accounts of rules to avoid breaches, preparing financial reports such as client balances and monthly cashflow, supporting audits and accountants' reports, and billing and invoicing. Good legal cashiers have good attention to detail, a positive attitude and able to meet deadlines, some IT skills including excel and word, and are able to adapt to training on legal finance software.

Routes into the role

In terms of qualifications, no degree is necessary and this role is easy get into straight from college where routes include various apprenticeship opportunities, work experience and trainee programmes. Routes alongside apprenticeships include trainee or junior vacancies, internal progression such as joining a law firm in general administrative or legal secretary roles and progressing into the accounting department. There are multiple ways to get into this job which again ensures that this is accessible for everyone.
